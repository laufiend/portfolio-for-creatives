The Crow of Tonle Sap

Inspired by Aesop’s fable, The Crow and the Pitcher, this is a linocut print made during a 3rd year workshop at IDEA. The fable is about a thirsty crow who cannot reach the water inside of a pitcher. It fills the pitcher with rocks until the water level rises enough for it to drink.

Because my mother used to read me this fable from a Khmer language textbook, I gave this piece some Cambodian flair. The stilt house is a reference to the floating villages of Siem Reap, and the pebbles say “caw!” in Khmer script. The crow itself acts as the “pitcher”, containing the lake of Tonle Sap.

I was also inspired by Cambodian artist Morn Chear. I liked how he does houses in his block prints, so I borrowed that carving technique for the roof of my house. អរគុណ! And many thanks to Katharine and my instructor Zoë for your guidance and advice!

Process: Step 1

Sketch and transfer

After settling on a sketch, I made a clean sketch on printer paper with sharpie in the desired dimensions. Then I placed the paper drawing side down on the linoleum block. I transferred the drawing by rubbing on it with acetone.
